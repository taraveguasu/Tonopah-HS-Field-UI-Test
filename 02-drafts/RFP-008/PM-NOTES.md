## 1. The package title promises signage and striping the scope narrative never describes

The package is titled *"Site Demolition, Salvage, Earthwork, Asphalt Paving, Wet
Utilities, **& Site Signage & Striping**"*. The Scope of Work narrative contains no
signage or striping scope group — its only use of the word "signage" is *"hazard
signage, flaggers, and traffic control"* under wet utilities. Meanwhile TAB's
proposal states **"Price Excludes Stripe or Signs"** and their scope review agenda
adds **"Excludes track and field signage"**.

So the title says the package carries it, the narrative never scopes it, and the
awarded sub priced it out.

I did **not** write a signage and striping inclusion — that would be manufacturing
scope from a title. I also did **not** write it into the exclusions — that would
quietly narrow the subcontract to match a proposal, which the authority hierarchy
forbids. It is left out of both, and it needs your ruling.

**If signage and striping stays in RFP-008,** the sub has to be told so before
execution, because he has priced it out twice in writing. **If it moves,** it needs
a home — no other package's narrative describes it either.

## 8. Exhibit B is no longer referenced — and applying that rule surfaced three conflicts

Per your 08.11.26 ruling, Exhibit B — Basis of GMP is out of the DIRECTIVES list, and the one
scope item that cited it now states the obligation without naming the source:

> ~~"…in lieu of the cast-in-place concrete headwall detail, **per Exhibit B - Basis of GMP
> (07.01.26) Owner Accepted Alternate**."~~
> "Provide reinforced concrete pipe flared end sections at the field storm drain discharge in
> lieu of the cast-in-place concrete headwall detail."

Same obligation, no paper trail into an Owner-side agreement. Directives are also reformatted
to `Addendum #01` / `Clarification #01` / `Clarification #02`.

**Reading Exhibit B properly to do that turned up three items it excludes that this exhibit
currently includes.** These are not drafting errors — they are places where what CORE told
NCSD and what CORE is buying from TAB do not line up:

| Exhibit B excludes | RFP-008 currently includes |
|---|---|
| **Rip rap** | *"Supply and install rip rap at the storm drain outlet as indicated."* and *"Provide and install rip rap as indicated."* (The exhibit separately excludes only rip rap **at the swale**, as Landscaping's.) |
| **Ground cover, dust palliative, or stabilization of disturbed soils** | *"…provide dust palliative as indicated at completion of the work at each site."* and *"Provide soil stabilization at all graded slopes…"* |
| **Offsite haul-off of spoils generated by the work** | *"Relocate spoils to the existing stockpile location north of the High School…, and legally dispose off site of all excess spoils generated by this Scope of Work."* |

Each needs a call, and the two directions have different consequences:

- **If Exhibit B governs**, these come out of TAB's subcontract and need a home — or they do not
  get done, and the site is left without dust palliative and with spoils on it.
- **If the subcontract governs**, CORE is buying scope it excluded from its own GMP. That is a
  legitimate choice, but it is CORE's cost, not the Owner's, and it should be a decision rather
  than an accident.

The third one is the same stockpiling tangle as item 3 above, seen from a new angle: the
narrative forbids stockpiling, Exhibit B assumes it, and Exhibit B *also* excludes hauling the
excess away. I left the exhibit as written — relocate to the stockpile and legally dispose of
the excess — because that is what VE #10 bought and what makes the arithmetic land.

**Nothing here is changed in the exhibit.** Removing scope on my own reading of an Owner-side
document is exactly what your ruling is designed to prevent.
